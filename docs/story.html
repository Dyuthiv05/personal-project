0 · Why this problem is worth solving

0:30 – 2:30

Diabetes is one of the largest and most expensive chronic diseases in the US, its hospital patients come back more often than others, and the drugs that best protect them are still underused. That combination is a classic pharma market-research question.

What changed in treatment

In my dad's era, diabetes drugs were judged on one thing: lowering blood sugar. Two newer classes changed that. GLP-1 agonists (Ozempic, Trulicity, Mounjaro) and SGLT2 inhibitors (Jardiance, Farxiga) also cut heart attacks, heart-failure hospitalizations and kidney decline in large trials. The American Diabetes Association now recommends them for people with type 2 diabetes and heart disease, heart failure or kidney disease regardless of their blood-sugar level [4].

The gap

Uptake has lagged the guidelines. Among US adults with type 2 diabetes newly diagnosed with heart disease, only 17% started either class within a year in 2022, up from 5.9% in 2018 [5]. The patients these drugs help most, older patients with heart and kidney disease who keep returning to hospital, are the ones a brand most needs to reach.

Cleaning the hospital file

Each step and why it matters for the readmission rate
  • Removed deaths and hospice discharges. Those patients can't be readmitted, so leaving them in understates the rate.
  • Kept one stay per patient. Frequent flyers would otherwise count many times and leak between training and test data.
  • Grouped ~700 diagnosis codes into 9 categories, and mapped 21 drug columns to classes using the NLM RxClass API.

7 · Recommendation

13:15 – 15:00

Limitations

  • Medicare Part D is mostly patients 65+; commercial patients may look different.
  • CMS hides rows under 11 claims, so new, low-volume adopters are undercounted.
  • The hospital data is 1999–2008 and observational: patterns, not causes.
  • Headroom compares prescribers with look-alikes; a low share can be the right call for a doctor's patients.

Next steps

  • Add commercial claims (IQVIA, Symphony) to cover under-65 patients.
  • Build a physician referral network from shared patients to find influencers.
  • Survival analysis of time-to-adoption after a launch.
  • Pilot in Florida and Ohio with matched control prescribers.
